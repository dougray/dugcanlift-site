Set Up LIFT Coach

LIFT Coach collects the training and eating your clients send from LIFT and lays it out as a roster you can read on a Monday morning — who trained, who ate to their numbers, and who has gone quiet. It's free, and there's no account to make.

You can use it right now in a browser tab at dugcanlift.com/coach without installing anything. Installing it just gives you an icon to tap and lets it open full screen and work offline.

On a computer

Probably where you'll actually read this — a roster is easier on a big screen. Works in Chrome or Edge.

  1. Go to dugcanlift.com/coach
  2. Look at the right-hand end of the address bar for the install icon — a small monitor with a downward arrow
  3. Click it, then click Install

If you don't see the icon, open the ⋮ menu in the top right, then Cast, save and share → Install page as app. Safari on Mac can do the same from File → Add to Dock.

On iPhone or iPad

This only works in Safari — if you normally use Chrome, open Safari instead just for this one step.

  1. Open Safari and go to dugcanlift.com/coach
  2. Tap the Share button — the square with an arrow pointing up, at the bottom of the screen (top of the screen on iPad)
  3. Scroll down the list that pops up and tap Add to Home Screen
  4. Tap Add in the top right corner

A native iPhone app

There's a Coach built as a real iPhone app too. You can't install it. It's signed with a free Apple developer account, which runs it on a phone wired to the Mac that built it and nowhere else — no App Store build, no TestFlight, nothing to send you. It's written up here because it exists, not as an offer. No date on it.

What it does today:

  • The same roster, flagging anyone who hasn't logged for seven days or more
  • The same charts — training volume, calories against goal, bodyweight, estimated one-rep max per lift — and the session log down to the individual set
  • A Cook tab: recipes, a week of meals built for one client, and the shopping list that falls out of it
  • A Train tab: workout templates put on days and sent back to that client's LIFT as a plan link
  • Its own Save Backup and Restore from Backup, and it reads a backup from the browser version to bring your recipes and workouts across

Getting a client's log into it is a paste, not a tap. The signing it uses can't claim dugcanlift.com links the way the Android app tries to, so tapping the button in their email opens the browser version instead — open the email, copy the link out of it, and paste it into Coach's Paste a Link.

On Android

  1. Open Chrome and go to dugcanlift.com/coach
  2. Tap the ⋮ menu (three dots stacked vertically) in the top right
  3. Tap Install app (some phones say Add to Home screen instead)
  4. Tap Install to confirm

A Coach icon shows up alongside your other apps. It's the fire cross with COACH across it — LIFT's says LIFT, so you can tell them apart if you run both.

A native Android app

There is now a Coach built as a real Android app rather than a web page in a tab — same roster, same numbers, no browser around it. You can't install it yet. It has a release signing key now — made on 13 September, and the key's fingerprint is published in this site's App Links file — but no build has been cut from it, so nothing in this section is a download. It's written up here because it exists and the page shouldn't pretend otherwise. No date on it.

What it does today:

  • Takes a client's link by pasting it in, or by sharing it into Coach from whatever app it arrived in
  • Opens on the roster sorted quietest first, with a banner across the top naming anyone who hasn't logged for seven days or more
  • Per client, a weekly summary table — sessions, sets, volume, average calories, average protein, how often protein hit the goal, average steps
  • Charts for training volume, calories against their goal, bodyweight, and estimated one-rep max for each lift
  • A session log down to the individual set, a day at a time
  • A Connect screen holding your name and email, writing the invite text for a new client, with its own Save Backup and Restore from Backup

Tapping a client's link opens it straight into Coach rather than a browser. That works because this site serves a file naming the app's signing key, so Android knows the two belong together. Sharing the link into Coach from another app works as well.

Connecting your first client

Once per client, and then never again.

  1. In Coach, open the Connect tab
  2. Put in your name and the email address you want logs to arrive at
  3. Tap Copy invite and send that text to your client however you normally talk to them
  4. They put your address into LIFT's Coach card once, in the app

From then on it's one tap on their end. They tap Send to Coach, their mail app opens already filled in, and they hit Send. You get an email with a seven-day summary and a button — tap the button and their log opens in Coach. Once a week is plenty.

If a client hasn't installed LIFT yet, point them at the install page first.

Meal plans you send back

Mostly your clients send you their week and you read it. COOK is the return trip: build a week of meals for a client from real recipes, send it as a link the same way they send you their log, and it lands in their app as a plan they can shop from and cook from.

The shopping list comes with it, scaled to the servings you planned. And because LIFT stamps what it logs, you'll be able to see which of the meals you planned actually got cooked — not just whether they hit their protein.

Where this is built, plainly: the browser version has it now, under Cook and Train — recipes, a week per client, the shopping list, and workouts you put on days and send the same way. The iPhone app has all of it too, and nobody can install that one. The Android app has none of it, and there's no date on that.

Where your roster lives

On your device and nowhere else. There's no server behind Coach and no account, for you or your clients — the log travels inside the link itself, in the part after the # that browsers never send to a server. Their numbers go from their phone, through your inbox, to this device.

In the browser, "on your device" means your browser's own storage, and the trade for that is real and worth knowing up front: clearing your browser's site data wipes your roster. There's a Save a backup file button under Connect for exactly this reason. Use it now and again, particularly before you clear anything or move to a new machine — Restore from a backup file puts it all back.

The Android app keeps its roster in its own storage instead, one file per client, so clearing your browser's data doesn't touch it. Uninstalling the app does, and so does clearing its data from Android's app settings. It has the same two backup buttons for the same reason, and Android's own cloud backup is switched off deliberately — a client's log isn't something to hand to Google Drive on your behalf.

If a client reinstalls LIFT from scratch they arrive as a new person in your roster, because the app forgets its own id along with everything else. Remove the stale one; the history you already have from them stays. If they restored from one of their own backup files, though, the id comes back with it and they stay the same person — worth telling them to save one before they change phones.

What Coach needs to run

In a browser: any reasonably current one — Safari 16.4 or newer, Chrome 103 or newer, or anything more recent than those. Older browsers get a clear message rather than a blank screen.

The Android app needs Android 8.0 or newer, and no browser at all. It talks to nothing on the network — every number it shows came out of a link you gave it.

Either way: nothing to buy, no sign-up, and no limit on how many clients you keep.
