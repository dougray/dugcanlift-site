LIFT on Wear OS

LIFT on the watch itself. Log food from the bundled USDA library, and work through a training session set by set, with a rest timer and your heart rate on the screen. Pair it with LIFT for Android and the phone sends the day’s workout to your wrist, and the sessions you finish go back into your log on the phone. Pairing is optional: with no phone at all the watch still logs food, with no network connection of any kind, and draws the food log as a QR code you scan with the web version.

This one is genuinely awkward to install, and there is no way around it. Wear OS has exactly one route onto a watch — the Play Store — and LIFT isn’t there yet. So this is a sideload: you need a computer on the same Wi-Fi network as the watch, and about five minutes with a command line. If that sounds like more than you want to do, the phone app needs none of it.

What you need

  • A watch running Wear OS 3 or newer — Pixel Watch, Galaxy Watch 4 and up, TicWatch Pro 5. Wear OS 2 watches can’t run it
  • A computer with adb on it. It comes in Android platform-tools, a single download from Google — no Android Studio needed
  • The watch and the computer on the same Wi-Fi network
  • LIFT for Wear OS (.apk) — about 25 MB. It comes from the latest Wear OS release on GitHub, alongside a SHA-256 checksum

On the watch

  1. Open Settings, then System, then About, then Versions
  2. Tap Build number seven times. The watch tells you you’re a developer now
  3. Go back to Settings. There’s a Developer options entry that wasn’t there before — open it
  4. Turn on ADB debugging
  5. Turn on Debug over Wi-Fi. Give it a few seconds and it shows an address like 192.0.2.23:5555 — write that down

Put the watch on its charger for the rest of this. A watch that dozes off drops the connection mid-install.

On the computer

Replace the address with whatever your watch showed you, and the path with wherever the file downloaded.

adb connect 192.0.2.23:5555
adb install ~/Downloads/lift-wear.apk

After adb connect, the watch asks Allow debugging? — tap the checkmark. If you miss it the install fails with device unauthorized; run adb connect again and watch the screen this time.

A successful install prints Success. LIFT is now in the watch’s app list, alongside everything else.

When you’re done, turn ADB debugging and Debug over Wi-Fi back off. Nothing about LIFT needs them; they were only how the file got across.

Using it

The home screen lists Start workout, Log food, Export logged foods and Phone. When your phone has sent a workout, it sits at the top under its own name, with how many exercises and sets it has. A workout you haven’t finished shows as Resume workout instead, even if the app was closed in between.

Food. Search by voice or the watch keyboard, whichever your watch offers, then set the amount on the rotating bezel or crown in grams or ounces and pick a meal. The watch says Logged once it has it.

A workout. Each set shows what you were asked to do in the biggest digits on the screen, last time’s numbers when the phone sent them, and your heart rate once the watch has a reading. Log set fills in from the prescription; change whatever you actually did. Rest starts by itself when you log a set, and the watch buzzes when it runs out. A lift done one side at a time counts each side, so “3 × 8 each side” is six sets, and the set line says which side is next: 3/6 · L. Supersets and circuits go round by round, one set of each exercise before any rest, with the group shown over the name (“Superset A · A1”); there’s no rest before a drop set and fifteen seconds before a rest-pause set. A target with no weight shows no weight, and a set logged without one reaches the phone without one, not as a zero. Start workout with nothing sent is the same screens with no prescription.

The first time you open it, the watch asks to read your heart rate. Say no and everything else works the same; there’s just no heart rate on the screen. The watch keeps only the highest heart rate around each set, shown under the set as “max 142 bpm”, and the session’s average and highest. Those go to LIFT on your phone with the session and nowhere else; the rest of your heart rate is shown during the session and not saved.

Finish workout ends the session. It goes to the phone if one is paired and connected, and otherwise waits on the watch and goes the next time the two connect. It stays on the watch until the phone says it has it. With no phone paired, a finished workout waits on the watch until you pair one: the QR export carries food only.

Pair with your phone

This needs LIFT for Android 1.17 or later on the phone. Skip it and the watch works on its own, as above.

  1. On the watch, open LIFT, tap Phone, then Pair with phone. Allow Bluetooth when it asks
  2. On the phone, open LIFT and find the Watch card on Home. Tap Pair with my watch, and allow Bluetooth when Android asks (it calls it Nearby devices)
  3. Your watch appears on the phone by name. Tap it, then accept the Bluetooth pairing request on both
  4. Both screens show the same six-digit number. If they match, tap Yes, it matches on the phone and Yes, pair on the watch. If they don’t, you’re pairing a different watch — tap No on either and the pairing ends there

After that they reconnect by themselves whenever LIFT is open on the watch. With LIFT closed, the watch’s side of the link is off.

To send a workout, use the Watch card on the phone: Send today’s workout when a routine is scheduled for today, or send any routine from the list under it. On the watch, Get today’s plan asks the phone for whatever is scheduled today.

What crosses the link, and nothing else. From the phone: the workout’s name, its exercises and notes, the sets with their target weight, reps and RPE, and your last numbers for each exercise. From the watch: each set as you log it, and the finished session, which goes into your log, each with the highest heart rate around each set and the session’s average and highest. Your food log doesn’t cross it — that still comes back by QR — and neither does the rest of your heart rate.

Getting the log back

  1. On the watch, tap Export logged foods
  2. The watch draws your log as a QR code — several in sequence if you’ve logged a lot, captioned 1 / 3 and so on. Swipe between them
  3. On your phone or computer, open the web version and choose Scan from watch. Point the camera at the watch and work through the codes in order
  4. Swipe past the last code and the watch asks Scanned all 3? — tap Done — clear these once you’ve confirmed everything arrived, or Keep them to leave the log alone
  5. Done — clear these asks a second time before it does anything. That one can’t be undone

Nothing clears on its own. The watch has no way of knowing whether a scan actually worked, and clearing automatically would throw the log away every time one didn’t — which is also why the destructive tap is behind two confirmations and the safe one isn’t behind any.

Why it works this way

The normal way a Wear watch talks to its phone is the Wearable Data Layer, and that needs Google Play Services running on both ends. LIFT for Android carries none. Rather than pull Play Services in, the two talk over LIFT Link: a direct Bluetooth connection between your phone and your watch, encrypted, with no server in the middle. The watch still asks for no internet permission at all, so it could not send your log to the internet even if something in it wanted to. Bluetooth reaches the phone you paired and nothing else.

Without a phone, the QR code is the transport for food, and it’s a picture of a screen.

The food library is bundled inside the app rather than looked up — the same 7,793 USDA records the web version searches, well under 1 MB of the download. It’s why a watch that has never been paired to anything still has something to log from.

The watch keeps the most recent 200 entries, however old they are, and exports every one of them.

Is it the same app as on Apple Watch?

Mostly the same job, over different plumbing. Both log food from the bundled library with no phone and export it by QR, and both take a workout from the phone and run it with last time’s numbers, a rest timer and your heart rate, then send the finished session back. The Apple Watch app talks to LIFT for iPhone; this one talks to LIFT for Android.

The Apple Watch app also records runs and hikes on GPS, saves workouts to Apple Health, and sends food logged from your recent foods to the iPhone. The Wear app does none of those: it records no runs, writes to no health app, and its food log leaves only by QR. The Wear app shows left and right for a lift done one side at a time, and supersets, drop sets and rest-pause sets, which the Apple Watch app doesn’t yet. See the full comparison.
