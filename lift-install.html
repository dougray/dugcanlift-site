Join The Test

LIFT is early, and it's built better with real people actually using it — not just me. Install it, use it the way you'd normally track food and training, and tell me what breaks, what's confusing, or what you wish it did. That feedback goes straight into what gets built next.

Nothing's on the App Store or Play Store yet. That's normal for something this early — it just means installing it looks a little different depending on your phone. No account, no password, nothing to download from anywhere sketchy.

On iPhone or iPad

This only works in Safari — if you normally use Chrome, open Safari instead just for this one step.

  1. Open Safari and go to dugcanlift.com/lift
  2. Tap the Share button — the square with an arrow pointing up, at the bottom of the screen (top of the screen on iPad)
  3. Scroll down the list that pops up and tap Add to Home Screen
  4. Tap Add in the top right corner

A LIFT icon now shows up on your home screen. Tap it like any other app from now on.

On Android — quick test

The fastest way to try it out, no download and nothing to allow. This is a lighter version — manual entry only, no automatic step tracking.

  1. Open Chrome and go to dugcanlift.com/lift
  2. Tap the ⋮ menu (three dots stacked vertically) in the top right
  3. Tap Install app (some phones say Add to Home screen instead)
  4. Tap Install to confirm

LIFT now shows up with your other apps. Tap it like any other app from now on.

On Android — full app

If you want the real thing — automatic step tracking, barcode scanning, everything — install the actual app instead of the web version. Takes a couple extra taps the first time, worth it if you're testing seriously.

  1. On your phone, go to dugcanlift.com/lift/install and tap Download LIFT for Android
  2. Open the downloaded file. Android will warn you it's outside the Play Store — tap Settings in that warning, then turn on Allow from this source (wording varies slightly by phone), then go back and open the file again
  3. Tap Install
  4. Tap Open once it's done

That "unknown source" warning is just Android being cautious about anything outside the Play Store — normal for pre-release testing, and only needs approving once.

Why isn't this in the App Store?

It's early and I'd rather get it in front of real testers now than wait months on a store review process. Installing it this way is the same app, just skipping the store — your data stays on your device either way, and there's nothing to buy or sign up for.
