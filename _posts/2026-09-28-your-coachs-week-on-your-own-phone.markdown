Last week Coach learned to show a trainer what they booked beside what you
logged. This week the same thing arrives on the other side of the link: on your
phone, on Train, your coach's week beside your own. Plus the meals they booked
for you, two more chains in Road Food, and a name at the top of the card so you
know who sent it.

## Your coach's week, on Train

If someone coaches you, their plan and your log have always been two separate
things that never met. Train shows one day at a time, and it will not go past
today. So a session booked for Friday was legible on no screen until Friday
arrived, and a Wednesday you did train was already behind you by Thursday.

Train now carries a card for the whole week: which days were booked, which you
logged, and inside a day, what was asked above what you did.

**A day still ahead says `to do`, and shows the work.** Not a zero, not a blank,
not an absence. That is the part no other screen could give you: the sets and
reps for a day you cannot scroll to yet. A day behind you reads `logged` or
`not logged`, and a day you trained that nobody booked reads `not booked`, with
what you did on it.

It is a **week**, Monday to Sunday, rather than a mark on today, for that same
reason: the days you cannot reach are the ones worth seeing mid-week. The arrows
read the week either side. A lift done on another machine is paired and labelled
`Asked Barbell · logged Smith machine`. Sets are counted, never matched one to
one, because if you did three of four the app cannot say which one you dropped.

**You are not being graded.** No score, no percentage, no streak, no colour on a
day nothing was logged against, nothing carried from one week to the next. The
words are `logged` and `not logged`, the same ones your coach reads, never
"missed" or "skipped". Under every card:

> Your coach's plan beside your own log. What else the week held, only you know.

A week your coach booked nothing in is no card at all, rather than an empty
frame explaining itself. And nothing new travels for any of this: no new key in
the link, nothing extra stored, no new permission. The card reads the plan you
already accepted against the log already on your phone.

## The meals they booked, and nothing about what you ate

A coach can book meals as well as sessions. Those now show on the same card,
under the day they were booked for.

</figure>

The useful part is the same as for a session: **the dish booked for a day you
cannot reach.** Cook's plan shows seven days from today and Train shows one, so
a dinner booked for next Thursday was readable nowhere until you got there. A
week that books only food is now a card where before there was none.

Here is the deliberate absence, and it is the most interesting decision of the
week. **The card says nothing whatever about what you ate.** No tick beside a
dish you cooked, no count of the day's foods, no verdict on a meal slot, no
macros next to a booked dinner. Your phone does in fact know when a planned meal
was logged, and it is still not printed.

The reason is that a tick on some meal rows and a blank on the rest is a score
with the numbers filed off. Nobody reads "Dinner, no tick" as a neutral fact.
And you are the one person on earth who already knows what you ate today.
Reading your own food log back to you in the third person, under a heading with
your coach's name on it, tells you nothing you did not know and turns a day you
ate differently into a mark against you. Your food log is on the Food screen,
dated, where it always was.

Your coach's copy does do the other half. Their Booked card names the foods you
stamped with that slot, above a count of the day's foods, under a note saying it
cannot know whether the dish was the one they booked. That is a trainer reading
about someone else and needing to be told the limits of it. This is you reading
about you.

Booked meals are on the **browser version only** so far. The phones show booked
sessions today and the meals are next.

## Signed, and matched to the right session

Two smaller things that both come down to the card telling the truth about
where something came from.

**The week is signed with your coach's name.** `From Dana`, or `From Dana ·
Doug` if two people booked one week, at the top of the card. A plan that carried
no name reads `From your coach`, exactly as every plan accepted before this
does. The browser has printed it since the card shipped; both phones dropped the
name on the way in and now keep it.

**Starting a booked session records which booking it answered.** Before, pressing
Start on a coach-scheduled workout wrote an ordinary session and nothing
remembered the two were related, so a second session you logged that day got
folded into the comparison. Now the booking remembers, and the extra session is
listed on its own as also logged instead. A routine you start from your own
library is never marked: a template is reusable, and starting one must not use
up a booking. That one is on Android.

## The card, said out loud

Both cards are built from short lines with a `·` between their clauses. On
screen that is a comma that takes up no vertical space. Through a screen reader
it is either the name of a character or nothing at all, and either way
`Mon 28 Sep · Lower A · logged · 1 meal booked` arrives as four unrelated
fragments. `L 3/3 · R 2/3` arrives as no language whatsoever — on the one line
of the card that says something about your body.

Both cards now compose a spoken version of every line beside the written one.
`·` becomes a comma, the sign between a weight and its reps becomes "by",
`3/3` becomes "3 of 3", `L` and `R` become "left" and "right", and
`Mon 28 Sep` becomes `Monday 28 September`, so the day row above is read as one
sentence:

> Monday 28 September, Lower A, logged, 1 meal booked. Opens this day on Train

It is composed from the same parts the written line is composed from, never by
substituting characters in the finished text — that would have to guess whether
the `x` in a lift you named yourself is a multiplication sign.

Four smaller things turned up on the way:

- **What you were asked to do and what you did are a comparison**, and read
  apart they are two lists of numbers with nothing between them. They are one
  announcement now, under the lift's name, which stays a heading so you can
  still move lift to lift.
- **LIFT's week arrows were `‹` and `›` and nothing else**, so their accessible
  name was a quotation mark. They are "Previous booked week" and "Next booked
  week", and an arrow with nowhere to go says so rather than only looking
  greyed out.
- **A day row said it could be pressed, but not whether it was open** — the
  card drew that only by whether more lines followed it — or that opening a day
  Train can show also moves Train there. Both are stated now.
- **Coach's By day / By lift chips showed which was selected in colour alone.**

Nothing on screen changed: not a string, not a pixel. And nothing is given
emphasis aloud that it does not have on the page. `not logged` is as flat
spoken as it is written, because the card counts and does not grade — and that
is either true in both forms or it was never true at all.

## Taco Bell and Sheetz

Road Food covers **13 chains** now, 19 more items than last week.

</figure>

Both of these were on the list of chains left out, for the same reason: they
publish their nutrition through a calculator rather than a table. That turned
out to be the wrong way to read it. The calculator is embedded in the chain's
own nutrition page, and a chain embedding a grid on its own site is the chain
publishing its figures. Looking the same numbers up in somebody's food database
is still out, and always will be.

So the rule now names the difference, and the quarterly checker enforces it.
Every run it opens the chain's own page, confirms the embed is still there, and
says so in the report before it reads a single number from it. If a chain drops
the embed, the report says so and a person goes and looks.

Some items were left out, for reasons worth knowing. A row that prints `< 1` for
sugar publishes neither a number nor a blank, so it stays out until it prints
one, and four rows across the two chains went that way. Sheetz's grid also has
build-your-own starting points named like finished sandwiches, carrying only the
meat and no roll: logging one of those as a sandwich would under-count a meal by
more than 200 calories. None of them is in the list under a sandwich's name.
