I remember standing in a doctor's office, staring at words I never thought I'd have to process. Heart failure. Liver failure. Cirrhosis. Lung failure. Three diagnoses that felt like a death sentence—like my body was holding me hostage and there was no escape route. I thought that was it. That was going to be my life: medications, restrictions, and a slow fade into a version of myself I didn't recognize.

But here's what they don't tell you about rock bottom—you don't have to stay there.

180 pounds. That's not just a number for me. That's 180 pounds of proof that transformation isn't about perfection. It's about persistence. It's about showing up on days when the scale doesn't move. It's about celebrating victories that nobody else can see.

The irony of weight loss is that the scale is the worst teacher. Don't get me wrong—I weigh myself. But somewhere around week three of my journey, I learned that the scale is a liar by omission. It doesn't tell the story of what's actually happening. It doesn't show you the strength you're building. It doesn't reflect the clarity in your eyes or the energy in your step. It can't measure the fact that you climbed a flight of stairs without gasping for air, or that you played with your kids without getting winded, or that you walked past the drive-thru and actually felt good about it.

This road has not been straight.

There have been weeks where the scale went up despite my best efforts—water retention, hormones, or just the universe testing my commitment. There have been moments where I ate pizza at midnight and felt that familiar shame creeping in. There have been training sessions where my body said no, and rest days that became rest weeks. This isn't a highlight reel. This is real life, and real life is messy.

But I'll tell you what happened in the mess: I crossed a 5K finish line. Then an 8K. Then a 10K. My lungs, which once told me they were failing, carried me across those distances. My heart—the one they said was giving out—pumped strong and steady as I proved to myself that I was still in there, still capable, still alive.

And the diagnoses? Three of them have been cleared. Gone. Not because I woke up one morning and decided to be sick—but because I woke up every morning and decided to fight. To choose the healthier option. To do the workout even when I didn't feel like it. To measure my progress not just in pounds, but in what my body could do.

Sleep apnea still has its hooks in me, but we're getting close. Every pound lost is another step toward breathing freely at night, and I can taste that victory already.

Now I'm setting my sights on a half marathon. That alone would have been unimaginable three years ago. But I'm not stopping there. My first Hyrox race is on the horizon, and this body that I was told was failing—it's going to complete an obstacle course race. Me. The person they told would need to manage declining health. The person who was supposed to resign themselves to this life.

I'm going to be sharing this journey with you—the unglamorous parts and the victories. My meal plans (the ones that work and the ones I had to adjust). My workout splits and the days I had to scale back. The mental battles and the breakthroughs. Because this isn't going to be a pretty road. There will be stumbles. There will be plateaus. There will be days when I wonder if it's worth it.

But here's what I know for certain: it's worth it.

This path isn't straight, but it leads somewhere real. It leads to a life where I get to be present. Where I get to move without pain. Where I get to dream bigger. And most importantly, it leads to a place where I chose myself—every single day, in a thousand small ways—and never gave up.

The road continues. And I'm inviting you to walk it with me.

Because this is happening. One way or another, this is happening. Not because the journey is perfect, but because I refuse to stop.

To those just starting: You are not what the scale says. You are not your diagnoses. You are what you do when nobody's watching. You are the person who shows up, day after day, choosing differently. That's everything.
