## Why repaint

The games played well, and the people who tried them said so. But the art was mostly drawn in
code: flat shapes, simple outlines, the look of a prototype. Cloud Dancer's airship was the
exception. It came from the painted app icon, and next to it everything else looked unfinished.
So the rest of the fleet got the same treatment.

## Cloud Dancer

Every enemy, boss, coin, chest and gun crate is now hand-painted art in the same style as the
airship: brass-banded zeppelins, gyrocopters with glass cockpits, balloon mines, flak towers on
rocky islets, and four bosses. The moving parts are still animated over the paintings: rotors
spin, gun turrets swivel, the Tempest Engine's lightning crackles, and the Brass Kraken's
clockwork tentacles sway under its riveted dome.

and a Sky Market full of upgrades like exploding shells, a five-ball broadside and a wingman
gyrocopter.

## Jolly Rogue

Jolly Rogue is a pixel-art game, and the pixel pirates stay; they have far too many frames of
running, jumping and shooting to repaint. What changed is the world behind them. Each of the four
levels now has a painted backdrop: a tropical island at golden hour, a sunset sea full of merchant
ships, a lantern-lit cargo hold and a candlelit gun deck.

## How it was made

I wrote detailed prompts for every enemy, boss and backdrop and generated the art in ChatGPT, a
whole sheet of objects at a time on a plain background. Then Claude cut each object out, sized it
to match the game's hit boxes, flipped the ships that came out facing the wrong way, and wired it
in, so the art is new but the games still play exactly the same.
